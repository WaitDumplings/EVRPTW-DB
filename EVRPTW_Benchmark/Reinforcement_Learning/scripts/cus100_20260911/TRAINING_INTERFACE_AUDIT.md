# Cus100 trainer audit, 2026-09-11

This is a static audit plus historical evidence, not a new training result.

The four REINFORCE methods expose `--training-epochs` as logical optimizer updates, `--physical-batch-size`, `--effective-batch-size`, and `--samples-per-instance`. Automatic weighted microbatch accumulation preserves the logical instance batch. The fixed ordered stream is required for budgets larger than the source pool. TERRAN exposes `--n-traj` and physical environments; its PPO update epochs, transition minibatches, step chunks, logical environment batches and gradient accumulation are distinct quantities and must all be recorded.

The approved current objective is `rivian_energy_vehicle_cost_v2`: `C_USD = 0.151750972762646 * D_km + 413.6331536717643 * K`. The source/config has no separate driver-hours term. The fixed vehicle coefficient may not be relabelled as an independently verified vehicle-plus-driver decomposition. Positive reward division does not alter the objective. Road uses the frozen Cus100 scale 432.7147451224062 USD; the final synthetic corpus uses its separately calibrated training-only shared scale 6530.963114223516 USD and terminal failure base 3.371478213719905. All five E methods use that same E contract. Method auxiliary training penalties do not become reported USD cost.

The current TERRAN canonical configs already contain Smooth L1 value loss, `vf_coef=0.1` and `critic_backbone_grad_scale=0.1`. Retaining the existing adapted method means explicitly retaining these values; this round does not invoke the separate `stable_cli` algorithm or introduce a new critic study. RRNCO retains the latest approved full model: stable AFT, nearest distance sampling, relation temperature 5, relation chunk 32, checkpoint bias, leave-one-out baseline, learning rate 0.0001. This is the currently adapted RRNCO, not a new node-only ablation.

## Historical EVRPTW-RL failure diagnosis

Source run: `results/DRL_rq_v1/E/Full-support/evrptw_rl/Cus100/seed_1234/16d0c7b4f59b2d2c532a9bfefc5c6fbaa7069cd6`.

The old 120-step training almost always exhausted the cap. Around 87% of customers were served and there were about 27 station visits per trajectory. All 4,100 logical updates reached that cap. Validation had a longer 180-step cap, more sampling candidates (100 in the stopped run), and independent route replay. It does not repair environment-failed trajectories into successful candidates: route export may append a depot conventionally, but the selector preserves failure. Thus a feasible unchanged validation score is not evidence of healthy 120-step single-trajectory training.

Pre-clipping gradient norm was 464.497 at update 1, 1192.960 at update 2, 0.0000502 at update 50, 0.00000267 at update 100, 0.00000862 at update 1000 and 0.002465 at update 4100. Thus optimizer entry was active, while gradients rapidly became tiny. Native Structure2Vec sum aggregation over 121 nodes followed by tanh scoring is a possible saturation mechanism; this remains a hypothesis, not an established interface bug. No aggregation/architecture change is authorized by this audit.

The rollout-baseline comparison occurred 31 times from update 1100 to 4100. All paired t-tests were NaN, consistent with identical actor and baseline greedy costs; it was not an omitted EVRPTW-RL scheduling call. The old DRL-TS fixed-update adapter never scheduled an update, despite the native `DRL_TS/train.py` path already comparing its actor and baseline once per `batches_per_epoch` (default 250) optimizer updates. This round restores that existing native adapter schedule in the fixed-update path. Event metadata says `schedule_source=native_adapter`, and the resolved training signature freezes the interval and training-pool-only probe source; this is not asserted to be a published-paper interval. Old results retain their original records.

The new calibration uses a shared 240-step train / 360-step validation cap and 30 train/validation trajectories. Early stopping inside completed trajectories still applies. This removes the known insufficient old cap without changing feasibility rules, architecture or objective. Probe checkpoints must remain disposable. Native optimizer parameter deltas, finite losses, training completion, verifier outcomes and peak process GiB are recorded; two successful updates are not a long-run robustness or convergence claim.

## Memory-only recomputation

EVRPTW-RL now accepts optional `--activation-checkpoint-stride`: 0 keeps previous execution, 1 recomputes every differentiable decoder step, 2 every other step. This is non-reentrant activation checkpointing with full recurrent gradients and RNG preservation; evaluation and no-grad baselines bypass it. No hidden state is detached, no candidate is dropped, no objective is reweighted, and model state-dict keys are unchanged. Three equivalence tests cover strides 1, 2, 3 with identical routes, RNG and log likelihood and matching all parameter gradients; the full EVRPTW-RL test file passes 16 tests. The resolved training signature records recomputation stride and semantics.

Synthetic E Cus100 demonstrated that native DRL-TS batch 5, while safe on short completed Road trajectories, can exceed 11 GiB during the hard-stage 240-step rollout. DRL-TS therefore also supports optional decoder-only activation checkpointing with the same stride flag. The encoder is evaluated once, including all BatchNorm updates; only the stateless decoder's intermediate tensors are recomputed. Four soft/hard equivalence cases confirm identical routes, RNG, log likelihood and every encoder buffer, with matching full parameter gradients. All 13 DRL-TS model/rollout tests pass. Formal manifests must declare the selected stride for both E and G. Preliminary E memory probes with the old Road reward scale remain explicitly tagged `preliminary_oldscale` and are excluded from final source-specific reward and quality gates.

## Initial synthetic feasibility is a quality result

The final TERRAN E CSV confirms the new scale, 6530.963114223516, in all three smoke epochs with finite policy/value losses; customer service declined 91.16 to 79.59 and almost all trajectories hit 240 steps. This is an observed early policy regression, not proof of convergence or a promise of later recovery. The final E DRL-TS hard stage succeeds on about 63–83% of sampled trajectories, and RRNCO reaches 86.9% after ten updates. These use the same source and canonical contract. Separately, the 500 training-only dual-verified reference routes require 107–199 actions (median 124, p95 164), all below 240. Therefore the available evidence does not show a universal adapter/unit error or a cap that excludes these feasible references. No new architecture or reward weights were introduced in response to the short quality results.
